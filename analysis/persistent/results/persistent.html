Inherited blind spots

Every probe the two experiments produced was scored on all 866 eval_sets/hu_ha rows. 39 rows are misclassified by all 45 of them — and the set barely moves when you change the attacker model, the memo condition, or the experiment. It was already there in the probe all five runs started from.

01

The errors are shared, not idiosyncratic

Each probe gets 23.6% of the eval set wrong. If those errors were independent across the 45 probes, the number of rows every one of them misses would be 5e-26. It is 39. The 9196 errors land on just 502 distinct rows, and 364 rows (42%) are never missed by anything.

03

It was inherited, not discovered

All five runs start from the same probe. The probe_iter0 pickles differ only in metadata — their scores are identical to 0e+00 — because every run trains its first probe on the same 50 base samples under the same pinned ensemble seeds. That shared probe makes 234 errors, and 93% of the universal core was already wrong there.

Of those 234 inherited errors, 66 are fixed by every arm and 39 by none. Retraining on red-team data shrinks the error set without relocating it — and in one arm it does not even shrink.

gpt-oss-120b takes its probe from 234 eval errors down to 111 over five retrains. deepseek-v4-pro ends at 299 — worse than the probe it started from.

04

Most of the core is the 0.5 threshold

These probes are shifted. They call 31% of eval rows positive against a 50% base rate, their median score is 0.053, and 90% of every error is a false negative. Move each probe to its own median score — the balanced-accuracy cut, since every split is exactly class balanced — and every arm gains.

Are these rows learnable at all?

The distinction that matters: is the core intrinsically ambiguous, or is it what red-team training data fails to cover? Two controls answer it, both single linear_then_softmax heads on the same activations. dev-only trains on the 218-row dev pool alone; ceiling is 5-fold cross-validation inside the eval set plus that pool, every row scored by the fold that did not train on it (mean AUROC 0.9865).

One thing found along the way

41 conversations appear in more than one eval split — 82 rows, 9.5% of the eval set, 40 of them shared between ant_hh and balanced_refusal. Labels always agree, so nothing is contradictory, but the per-split eval means are not independent of each other, and the 39 core rows are really 35 distinct conversations.

Related, from the labelling side: an eval conversation whose assistant refuses is labelled harmful 0% of the time (n=44) against 53% for one that complies — and refusing rows are almost never missed, at a mean of 1.0 of 45 probes wrong. None of the 39 core rows contains a refusal. The probes have learned refusal as the negative class; the core is what is left when that cue is absent.

45 probes — experiment22's two arms (6 iterations each) and experiment23's three (11 each), the latter read out of the experiment23_cloud branch — scored on all 866 rows of the four eval_sets/hu_ha splits. Scoring uses the cached full-split activation blobs with PROBE_FUSED_ENSEMBLE=0, as the runs did: the matrix reproduces every published comparison CSV to 4.5e-04 AUROC, which is what licenses a claim about individual rows. No LLM is loaded and no activation is recomputed at any point.

The run probes are 10-member score-averaging ensembles; the two controls are single heads at seed 42, early-stopped on the ceiling study's reserved 25% dev slice, so they read against that study's curves rather than against the runs' comparison CSVs. Every number here is generated from analysis/persistent/results/summary.json by build_artifact.py.
