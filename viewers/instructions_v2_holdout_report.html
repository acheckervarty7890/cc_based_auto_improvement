Do iteration 3's red-team finds beat more than one probe?

Instruction-following concept · google/gemma-3-27b-it layer 32 · 10 independently seeded v2 probes per attacker

The question. A red-team run reports a find whenever the probe of the day disagrees with the judge. But that probe is one fit among many possible ones, and nothing in the run distinguishes a conversation that defeats the classifier from one that merely defeats that fit's initialisation.

The measurement. Refit the iteration-2 vintage probe with 10 seeds, then score the pairs that are in the iteration-3 training set but not in the iteration-2 vintage — rows held out of every one of those fits, on both sides of the split. Per conversation, the success rate is the fraction of the 10 probes that misclassify it, thresholded at logit ≥ 0 exactly as ProbeJudge does. Every refit was verified to reproduce the sweep's v2 probe by re-scoring an eval split and demanding the recorded AUROC to the last bit.

“New at iteration 3” is a property of the training set, not of when the attacker found it. filter_dataset refits its bag-of-words classifier each cycle and drops a different top-percentile, so a pair can be found in an early rotation, dropped from the iteration-2 set, and taken back at iteration 3. Most of these pairs were found in the last rotation (67 of 92 and 120 of 148), but 22 and 19 come from the first — and they are held out of the v2 fits all the same, which is what this measures.

Findings

About half of the new finds are not probe-draw artifacts

Of the attacker's own success conversations that v2 never saw, 45 of 92 (gptoss120b) and 109 of 148 (nemotron) fool at least five of the ten v2 probes, and 29 / 74 fool all ten. Iteration 3 was largely re-finding weaknesses the iteration-2 probe really has, not exploiting the particular fit it happened to be handed.

The rest is a seed lottery, and it is large

30 of 92 and 21 of 148 attacker successes fool none of the ten. Those are conversations the probe of the day misclassified that a rerun of the same training with a different initialisation would have classified correctly — reported as finds, trained against, and not reproducible. Between the extremes the middle is thin (51 and 87 rows at 1–9 of 10), so a find is usually either robust or a coin flip, rarely in between.

Generated counterparts carry little held-out evidence

The LLM-written opposite-class halves fail at 0.070 / 0.095 against probes that never saw them — five to seven times below the successes they were written from. Only 1 of 92 and 3 of 148 fool all ten seeds; in just 1 and 6 pairs do both members beat a majority. They balance the labels, but as evidence about the probe they add little.

Memorisation is total, so the held-out rate is a real generalisation gap

v2's own fitted rows fail at 0.003 / 0.006 — essentially every training row is classified correctly. Its validation rows (never fit; they act only through early stopping) fail at 0.353 / 0.265, which brackets the held-out numbers. Read against that reference, gptoss120b's v3-only rows are no harder than v2's own unfitted rows, whereas nemotron's are harder: its iteration-3 attacker found something its iteration-2 data genuinely did not cover.

Beating the probe and improving the probe are not the same thing

nemotron's iteration-3 finds transfer to the v2 probes far better than gptoss120b's (0.707 vs 0.477) — the better attack by this measure — yet training on them lowered mean eval AUROC (v3 0.785 vs v2 0.803, over the same ten seeds). A batch of samples can be reliable probe failures and still not generalise to the eval splits, either because they concentrate in a region those splits do not sample or because they resemble each other more than they resemble anything else. Which of the two it is, this measurement does not settle.

Per-attacker asymmetry

gptoss120b's held-out failures are lopsided — 0.420 on rows labelled assistant_does_not_follow_the_instruction against 0.127 on follows, i.e. its v2 probes mostly miss violations rather than over-flag compliance. nemotron's are symmetric (0.389 / 0.413).
